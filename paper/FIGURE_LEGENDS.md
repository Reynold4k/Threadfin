# Figure legends

## Figure 1 | Threadfin connects receptor identity to B-cell state relationships

**Paired inputs and integration (left):** the compact three-colour scRNA-seq cloud illustrates expression-state clusters. Original membrane-BCR and heavy/light V(D)J-contig glyphs depict receptor sequencing. Three separately rooted miniature trees, each with a different family colour, illustrate sequence relationships within distinct receptor families; they are neither measured phylogenies nor ancestry links between families. Their colours encode receptor membership, not RNA-state labels, and no one-to-one correspondence between RNA clusters and receptor families is imposed. Threadfin defines families within donors using IGH V/J and junction similarity, with optional light-chain refinement. A cartoon threadfin fish represents integration: the paired measurement paths join an incoming fin filament, and two outgoing filaments connect the resulting conceptual landscapes. The filaments are workflow connectors, not biological lineage edges. The belly's double-peak sketch represents RNA distributions. Its Fréchet-mean label describes the unshrunk averaging stage: the mean of a family's RNA feature vectors minimises squared feature-space distances. The default kernel profiles additionally use context adjustment, reliability-dependent shrinkage and projection; the drawing does not imply an optimal-transport solver. The user-approved fish illustration is an AI-generated PNG based on a supplied fish reference, embedded with its transparent background and original aspect ratio. Scientific plots and receptor schematics remain vector artwork. The workflow connections are drawn as tapered fin extensions, with pale gold and blue-green shading matched to the fish.

**Branched GC landscape (upper right):** every circle denotes one illustrative family. Colour indicates its dominant captured RNA state, and point area is proportional to a synthetic captured-cell count. Some central families are drawn larger to illustrate expansion; central position does not imply greater clone size in empirical embeddings. A gold outline and aligned label emphasise the selection-associated node relevant to Figure 2's model-antigen GC analysis; the outline is an annotation, not a confidence region. The dashed return to cycling is a candidate recycling mechanism, not an estimated trajectory. Co-occupancy alone cannot distinguish forward progression from return, and direction requires independent lineage or informative temporal evidence. No memory-to-GC re-entry or future fate is demonstrated.

**Continuous landscape (lower right):** a separate, more linear pseudo-UMAP illustrates gradual clone-profile variation relevant to Figure 3's GC/output and maturation questions. A vivid blue–teal–amber–rose gradient represents an illustrative balance of cycling and output-associated programmes; intermediate colours indicate intermediate profiles, not separate categorical states. It introduces neither a selection node into infection data nor a temporal direction. All coordinates, counts, tree branches and compositions in this figure are synthetic. The maps are alternative patterns, not aligned samples or a pooled analysis; different mice are not linked into a single clonal time course. Map neighbourhoods represent profile similarity, not ancestry between families. Figure 2B's historical centroid reconstruction remains distinct from the default kernel-profile workflow illustrated here.

## Figure 2 | Model-antigen GC state nodes and independent reporter measurements

**A,** Designs of the independent NP-OVA and RBD cohorts of the GSE287123 H2B-mCherry reporter study. Division history, antigen-probe binding and GC-zone gates are measured labels from separate libraries/cohorts. **B,** Newly calculated GSE246382 NP-OVA day-14 embedding of 377 donor-restricted productive IGH V–D–J receptor groups (≥1 captured cell; 762 cells). These historical-style groups can merge distinct junctions: 83 contain multiple junction nucleotide sequences. They are distinct from the stricter 487 same-mouse sequence families retained in the parameter audit. Colour denotes Leiden clone_cluster; point area is proportional to captured-cell count. The reconstructed cell-UMAP group centres supply Euclidean distance-matrix rows to UMAP (40 neighbours, min_dist 0.65, seed 123), then Scanpy neighbours (15) and Leiden (resolution 0.3, seed 0). The display retains one 15-neighbour graph component and six clusters across seven seeds; median pairwise adjusted Rand index is 0.877 (minimum 0.701). Selection considered continuity and local fidelity; the historical k=20/min_dist=0.4 control is more seed-stable (median 0.989). Measured gates annotate LZ/DZ-associated regions (clusters 0/1), a Myc-positive-LZ-associated region (cluster 3: 80% Myc-positive LZ) and a plasma-cell-associated region (cluster 2: 97% PC); the other groups retain mixtures (S8B–E). Percentages are equally weighted means of receptor-group compartment fractions. Gates are not used to construct the graph; marker RNA reuses the expression data. The map does not establish temporal direction, two future fates or shared clonal ancestry within every V–D–J group. **C,** GSE287123 RBD reporter cell UMAP (36,188 cells from ten mice, five per vaccine arm), coloured by the measured mCherry-high or mCherry-low gate. **D,E,** The same 381 reliable (reliability ≥0.5) donor-private IGH sequence families, coloured by captured mCherry-low fraction (at least six divisions in the 36-hour window; **D**) or mean V-region mutation frequency (**E**; colour scale clipped at 0.03). Family membership uses V/J and junction nucleotide sequence similarity within each mouse, rather than B's broader V–D–J grouping. Coordinates are the saved kernel-profile UMAP (15 neighbours, min_dist 0.1, spread 1, seed 0); point size increases with captured-cell count. The 85-setting audit supports a stable division association and a weak global linear SHM association, while retaining detectable local SHM structure. A flat-looking colour map does not establish absence of association. mCherry dilution measures recent divisions, whereas mean V mutation frequency measures accumulated divergence; neither their difference nor their correlation estimates mutation per division. Supplementary Figure 16 audits within-mouse associations, matched family/gate SHM contrasts and reliability-filtered capture coverage. Only extreme mCherry gates enter this comparison. Reporter and sort labels are external measurements; gate-specific libraries remain a potential confound. Coordinates are not aligned across the two studies. Selection/output interpretation is restricted to model-antigen GC and does not establish future fate or superiority over simpler family expression summaries.

## Figure 3 | Clone-state variation across *Plasmodium* infection

**A,** Compact design of the distinct early and later PcAS infection cohorts. Early samples broadly capture B cells; later samples enrich IgD-low B cells and include saline and anti-malarial arms, naive spike-in cells and uninfected controls. Each mouse is sampled terminally. **B,** Early-infection map of 801 reliable families, coloured by captured plasmablast (PB) fraction. **C,** Central, enlarged map of 1,183 reliable later-infection families, coloured by captured GC fraction. Saved coordinates use UMAP with 50 neighbours, min_dist 0.5, spread 1 and seed 0; the sampled pair-distance rank correlation to the original family features is 0.856 (default display, 0.764). Region labels describe GC-, PB- or memory-enriched profiles. B and C use separate cohorts and are not an aligned temporal trajectory. Point size increases with captured-cell count. **D,** Later-infection cell UMAP, containing 72,359 cells and coloured by annotated GC, PB, memory-like and other states. **E,** Cycling-state variation within 329 early families whose captured cells are all annotated PB. The x-axis is distance in the saved two-dimensional map to the mean coordinates of 66 reliable families containing at least 50% GC cells; the y-axis is the family dark-zone/cycling RNA module score. Day 7 contributes one family, day 10 contributes 227 and day 14 contributes 101. Within each of ten mice with at least five eligible families, the association is negative (median Spearman ρ=−0.596). Coordinate distance and module score reuse RNA information and describe captured state variation; they do not independently validate maturation time or GC origin. **F,** Per-mouse fraction of reliable families containing at least 50% GC cells, shown separately for saline and anti-malarial arms. The denominator is all reliable families within that mouse, without a UMAP-distance threshold. Dots represent 36 mice (three per arm/day); lines connect group medians. These mice contribute 1,174 families; the nine families from four naive control mice included in C are excluded from F. Differences in captured composition across terminal samples do not track a family across days or by themselves estimate a causal treatment effect. These maps describe family-state organisation, not ancestry, GC re-entry, selection nodes or future fate.

## Figure 4 | Lineage barcodes connect early clone states to later observations

**A,** The public LARRY in-vitro sampling design, with day-2, day-4 and day-6 observations. **B,** Author SPRING coordinates, showing a deterministic sample of 40,000 of 130,887 cells coloured by author state annotations; all annotated states are retained in the source data. **C,** Threadfin kernel UMAP of 7,575 expanded clone-day profiles from 49,302 uniquely barcode-labelled cells. Colour denotes the dominant captured state and point area increases with capture count. Three example barcodes are selected by the largest minimum capture count across all three days; lines join known barcode identities, and labels give sampling day. **D,** Day-6 neutrophil fraction versus out-of-fold prediction from day-2-only kernel profiles for 172 eligible barcodes, using the first five-fold partition. R² is computed across pooled out-of-fold predictions. **E,** Median and interquartile range of fold R² over three repeated five-fold partitions for six representations/controls and two observed state fractions. The unsupervised early-cell representation is transductive but excludes future cells. Repeats are not independent biological replicates; observed state composition is not complete developmental potential. The original dataset and Clonotrace study are cited in the main text.

## Figure 6 | Predictive value and sampling robustness are distinct method properties

**A,B,** Whole-mouse-held-out mCherry-low gate-fraction error in the NP–OVA and RBD reporter datasets. Each point is a mouse, and ticks give medians; lower MAE is better. All eight methods/controls retain the same eligible families and downstream readout procedure, with at least two measured cells per family. Representations are transductive and reporter labels are held out from the supervised model. **C,** Median mouse-wise R² for every measured readout at that same capture threshold. A value of zero is the constant-test-mean reference, not a permutation-derived chance level. The readout and its units are specific to this benchmark; RNA means remain the strongest division-fraction baseline. **D,** Sampling-dependent kernel-profile reliability in the LARRY clone-day analysis, with the 0.5 display threshold. **E,** Mean squared error of an unshrunk kernel mean and the Threadfin shrunken estimate against disjoint reference cells. Biological barcodes are split between calibration and evaluation; evaluation uses 250 clone-day-well profiles containing at least 16 cells, 20 random splits per profile and 2, 4 or 8 query cells. The reference uses a disjoint half of the cells and smoothing is disabled. **F,** Reduction in pooled mean error: 27.2%, 14.2% and 6.5% at 2, 4 and 8 query cells. Repeated splits and different dates/wells from one barcode are not independent biological replicates. This experiment measures estimator denoising, not absolute calibration of reliability or future fate prediction. Native comparison implementation and execution scope remain in Supplementary Figure 7.
