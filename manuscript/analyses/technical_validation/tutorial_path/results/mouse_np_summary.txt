Threadfin summary
=================
Cells with a BCR: 3,894 of 10,109, in 2,295 clones (470 clones have two or more cells and are profiled).

1. Are cells of one clone more alike than cells of different clones? (clonal coherence)
   Clone identity explains 11.7% of the variance of cell states (the expression embedding); with clone labels shuffled within 'sample', 4.0% (p = 0.00498, 200 permutations): an excess of 7.7 percentage points.
   Profiles are shrunk towards the average profile, so a clone's (smoothed) profile reaches reliability 0.5 at 9 cells; no clone is filtered out on reliability.

2. Which clones behave alike? (clonal programmes)
   No distinct programmes: no split of the 470 profiled clones into separate groups was significant (split test p = 0.43).

3. Do your labels explain how clones differ? (clone-level tests)
   Labels are shuffled among clones of the same donor and of similar size (2, 3-4, 5-9 or >= 10 cells), because small clones' profiles are pulled towards the average profile: an effect below cannot be explained by clone size alone.
   division_gate explains clone states: 7.7% of clone-profile variance vs 0.3% for shuffled labels (p = 0.0005, 402 clones; 68 clones without a majority level left out).
      This label is fixed within each sample: the comparison is between samples (n = 14), not between independent clones.
   isotype explains clone states: 1.5% of clone-profile variance vs 0.6% for shuffled labels (p = 0.0165, 447 clones; 23 clones without a majority level left out).

4. Do clones keep their state over 'division_gate'? (clonal memory; levels in the order mCherry-high -> mCherry-low)
   202 clones seen at >= 2 levels: memory index 0.35 [0.11, 0.82], p = 0.002 (1 = clones stay as distinct as they were, 0 = no more similar to their earlier self than to another clone of its donor seen at the same levels; sampling noise can put it outside 0-1).

Per-cell programme labels: adata.obs['clone_programme']; per-clone table: result.clones.
